Finding the Minimum Delay of a Server

A server is a kitchen. Requests are orders. Delay is how long a customer waits. We find the exact settings that make the wait as short as possible, using partial derivatives, the Hessian matrix, eigenvalues, and least squares. Then we check the answer three independent ways.

D(n,b) = A/n + B·n + C/b + E·b + k·n·b
🍳Server = KitchenThe computer that handles requests, like a kitchen handles orders.
🧾Request = OrderEvery click or API call is an order waiting to be cooked.
👩‍🍳n = Cooks (worker threads)Too few → orders pile up. Too many → cooks bump into each other.
🍕b = Batch sizeCook 1 pizza at a time → oven heated again and again. Wait for 30 → first customer starves.

Five parts of the waiting time

Each part of the formula is one everyday kitchen effect. Two of them get smaller when you turn a knob up, and two get bigger. That tug-of-war is why a "sweet spot" exists.

TermKitchen meaningTurn knob up →
A / nOrders pile up when there are too few cooksfalls ↓
B · nEach extra cook adds crowding and coordinationrises ↑
C / bOven warm-up is shared by the batch (more pizzas, less cost each)falls ↓
E · bThe first order waits for the batch to fillrises ↑
k · n · bMany cooks and big batches fight for the same counter space (memory)rises ↑

Units: A and C in ms·unit, B and E in ms per unit, k in ms per (cook × order). D is in milliseconds.

STEP 3b · NEWTON'S METHOD (calculus + a 2×2 linear system)

Each step solves H·Δ = ∇D and moves to x ← x − Δ. It starts from the k = 0 answer.

STEP 4 · IS IT REALLY A MINIMUM? (second-derivative test)

The Hessian matrix holds the second derivatives. If it is positive definite, the point is the bottom of a bowl. That means both eigenvalues are > 0, or equivalently Dnn > 0 and det H > 0.

The key idea of our method: measure the knobs in "doublings", not in steps of one.
Going from 1 to 2 cooks is a huge change, and going from 40 to 41 is almost nothing. So we work with u = ln n and v = ln b. On that scale each trade-off becomes a perfectly symmetric bowl:
A/n + B·n = 2√(AB) · cosh(u − u*), where u* = ln √(A/B)
A cosh curve is very close to a parabola, so a quadratic surface fits it almost perfectly. On the ordinary scale the same bowl is badly lopsided (a steep wall on the left, a gentle slope on the right), and quadratic fits miss. The ④ Proof tab tests this claim on hundreds of random servers.
① MeasureRun the server at a grid of 5×5 settings. Every reading has random noise, like a real stopwatch.
② FitLeast squares: solve the normal equations XᵀX·c = Xᵀy (6 unknowns) to get a quadratic bowl.
③ SolveSet the bowl's gradient to 0. This gives a 2×2 linear system H·x = −g.
④ VerifyEigenvalues of the fitted H > 0? Then it's a real bowl (a positive-definite quadratic form).
⑤ ZoomHalve the window around the bottom and repeat. Stop when the bowl is flatter than the noise.
